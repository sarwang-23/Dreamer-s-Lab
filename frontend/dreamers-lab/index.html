Who is it for

For the next generation of thinkers and builders.

Dreamers Lab is designed for students in Grades 9, 10, 11 and 12. It is suitable for:

  • Students curious about business, startups or innovation.
  • Creative thinkers who enjoy solving problems.
  • Students who have ideas but don’t know how to develop them.
  • Students who want to develop leadership and communication skills.
  • Students who want to explore entrepreneurship before choosing their future academic or career path.

No previous business knowledge or startup idea is required.

The 12-week Dreamers Lab journey

One cohort. Twelve weeks. A journey from curiosity to creation.

Proposed program structure for the initial three-month cohort.

Weeks 1–2

Phase 1 — Discover

Understand yourself. Explore the world. Students identify their interests, strengths and curiosities, explore entrepreneurial thinking and learn to notice problems worth solving.

Outputs: Personal interest map, observation exercise and initial problem ideas.

Weeks 3–4

Phase 2 — Ideate

Find a problem worth solving. Students explore different problems, select a target audience, brainstorm solutions and narrow their ideas using simple evaluation frameworks.

Outputs: Problem statement, target-user profile and initial solution concept.

Weeks 5–6

Phase 3 — Validate

Talk to people. Test your assumptions. Students learn to ask better questions, conduct simple customer interviews, understand existing alternatives and gather feedback before building.

Outputs: Interview insights, problem validation summary and refined idea.

Weeks 7–9

Phase 4 — Build

Bring the idea to life. Students create a low-cost prototype, mock-up, service demonstration or simple digital product. They receive feedback and improve their solution.

Outputs: Prototype, solution demonstration and feedback log.

Weeks 10–12

Phase 5 — Pitch & Present

Share what you built. Show what you learned. Students refine their business model, estimate basic costs, prepare a pitch and present their project to mentors, peers and invited guests.

Outputs: Final pitch deck, demonstration and next-steps roadmap.

What students create

Every Dreamers Lab participant finishes with something to show.

By the end of the cohort, students will have worked towards:

  • Identifying a meaningful real-world problem.
  • Defining who experiences that problem.
  • Speaking to potential users.
  • Developing a possible solution.
  • Creating and testing a prototype.
  • Understanding the basic economics of the idea.
  • Preparing and delivering a final pitch.
  • Reflecting on their strengths, learning and next steps.

Outcomes will depend on the student’s project and level of development. A successful learning experience does not require launching a registered business.

Why parents should care

Prepare your child for a world that rewards initiative.

Academic learning is important. But the ability to apply knowledge, work with others, communicate ideas and adapt to change matters too. Dreamers Lab gives students a structured opportunity to practise these skills in a practical setting.

Instead of only hearing about entrepreneurship, they experience the process of trying something, getting feedback and improving it.

Our promise to parents: We don’t tell students who they should become. We help them explore what they could become.

FAQs

Questions from students, parents and schools.

Does my child need a startup idea to join?

No. Students begin by exploring interests and identifying problems.

Does the student need coding or business knowledge?

No. The program is designed to introduce entrepreneurship from the ground up.

Will students start a real company?

The objective is to experience venture building. Some ideas may progress into real businesses, but registering a company is not a requirement.

Will students work individually or in teams?

The program can combine individual reflection with collaborative project work, depending on the cohort design.

What happens at the end of the program?

Students present their projects, reflect on what they learned and receive guidance on potential next steps.

Can a school enrol an entire group?

Yes. Schools can enquire about a cohort designed for their students.
