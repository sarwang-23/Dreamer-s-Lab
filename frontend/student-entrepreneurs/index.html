The opportunity

Your university years are a chance to experiment.

You have access to classmates, faculty, campus communities, emerging technologies and new perspectives. You can explore problems in the real world, build with a small team and test an idea before making a major commitment.

But many students face the same questions:

  • How do I know if my idea is worth pursuing?
  • How do I find people who have this problem?
  • How do I build an MVP without a large budget?
  • How do I find a technical or business co-founder?
  • How do I get my first users or customers?
  • How do I balance a startup with my studies?

Building Dreams provides a structured process to help you work through these questions.

How you will work

Build in focused sprints.

Rather than spending months discussing an idea without testing it, participants work through structured milestones. Each sprint has:

  • A clear objective.
  • A practical task or experiment.
  • A measurable output.
  • Mentor or peer feedback.
  • A decision about what to do next.

Example: a customer-discovery sprint

A customer-discovery sprint may focus on interviewing ten relevant potential users, identifying recurring problems and deciding whether to continue, refine or change the idea.

The numbers and milestones will be tailored to each venture rather than treated as universal success criteria.

The mentor advantage

Learn from people who’ve built, launched and operated.

Building Dreams aims to connect student founders with experienced entrepreneurs, functional experts and practitioners who can help them navigate real-world challenges. Mentorship can cover product, technology, marketing, sales, finance, operations, hiring and fundraising.

The objective is not to provide answers to every question. It is to help founders ask better questions and make better-informed decisions.

What success looks like

Progress is more important than a perfect pitch.

Depending on your starting point, success could mean:

Rejecting an idea before spending heavily on it.

Discovering a customer problem that matters.

Building a prototype.

Getting your first ten active users.

Running a successful pilot.

Making your first sale.

Finding a complementary co-founder.

Becoming ready for an incubator or accelerator.

Not every venture needs funding, and not every idea should become a company. The goal is to make evidence-based decisions and build with greater clarity.

FAQs

Before you begin.

Do I need a co-founder?

Not to begin. You can start by validating the problem and identify the skills you need as the idea develops.

Does Building Dreams provide funding?

Building Dreams focuses on venture development and guidance. Investor introductions or funding opportunities may be possible where relevant, but funding is not guaranteed.

Do I need a registered company?

No. Early exploration and validation can happen before company registration.

Can I join if I have already launched my product?

Yes. Support can be tailored to your stage, subject to the program’s scope.

Can I participate while studying full-time?

The program should be structured around manageable execution milestones. The final time commitment will be communicated before enrolment.
